# Kickoff brief: visitor access at Contoso HQ

*Contoso HQ is a fictional company. All names, rules and numbers in this packet are invented for the workshop.*

## Who we are talking to

The facilities team of Contoso HQ, a corporate campus with four buildings: Main Tower, East Wing, West Wing and Annex. About 800 people work there. A facilities administrator handles visitor access day to day. Security guards staff the lobby of each building.

## What happens today

- Visitors sign a paper log at the lobby.
- The guard phones the employee host to ask "Are you expecting someone?" The host often does not answer.
- Nobody can search the paper log. After a security audit last year, the facilities team spent two days reading pages.

## What the facilities team wants

1. The administrator creates a visitor pass before the visit: the building, the dates, and the visitor's name.
2. The pass is a QR code. A guard checks the QR code at the lobby and lets the visitor in, or not.
3. The facilities team can see who came in and when.
4. The facilities team can ban people. A banned person must never get in.

## How they described the solution

> "It has to be **simple**. Our guards are not technical, and IT cannot take on a new service this year."

> "We do not want to pay for a server for the pilot. If it works, we may think about one later."

## What the facilities team did not discuss

They did not talk about email, about visitors creating their own passes, or about sign-in. In a side conversation the administrator said that one day the QR code could be emailed to the visitor. That is not part of the first version. See `stakeholder-notes.md`.

## Success, in the team's words

"In three months, the paper log is gone, and any guard can tell in a few seconds if a visitor is allowed."
